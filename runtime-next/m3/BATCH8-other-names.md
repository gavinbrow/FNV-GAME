# Batch 8: other-name interval evidence

The Batch 7 selector reconstruction yields 305 skipped intervals, 88,561 total bytes and 33,011 nonzero bytes. These are interval-level counts, not declared object sizes or counts for any particular symbol family. The 70,591-byte interval beginning at 0x01000001 alone has 18,212 nonzero bytes; its captured declaration is a single `char`, so its interval tail must not be attributed to that declaration.

Among the 305 starts, 188 have a `_DAT_...` unit key, 17 have a `?_DAT_...` key, and 110 have an `UNK_...` key; categories can overlap. There are no `OFF_` or `PTR_` unit keys at these interval starts. `_DAT_...` is also the x86 C linker spelling for an `extern "C" DAT_...` source symbol; it is not evidence on its own of a source identifier beginning `_DAT_`. Source spellings at an address and unit keys are recorded independently; sharing an address is evidence of co-location, not proof of equivalent C++ linkage or type. The prior OFF attribution is unconfirmed.

`inventory/b8_other_names.json` records every interval, source spellings and captured declarations with paths and lines, unit keys, and a conservative disposition. Of these, 29 have a consistent, narrowly recognized primitive scalar `extern "C" DAT_...` declaration and corresponding C-decorated unit key, no captured alternative spelling or source definition, a nonzero scalar head, and no intersecting saved data-v3 field or relocation. Their declared heads total 44 bytes: 19 `char`, five `unsigned char`, three `float`, and two `int`. The candidate range is exactly that declared scalar extent, never the rest of the interval. They are review candidates, not selection-ready entries. All other intervals are deferred, including ambiguous declarations, unknown bounds, aliases, and potential zero heads. Captured extern declarations are lexical evidence, not a complete C++ parse; absence of a captured conflict is not proof of its absence.

Before integration, verify each candidate against current placements, rewritten directories and actual source linkage; compile the exact declaration and verify section, bytes, alignment and relocation behavior; then run full integrated checks and controls. This inventory does not establish that the listed rewritten-directory exclusions are exhaustive. No shared selector, generator, source, build or gameplay was changed or run. `b8_other_names_evidence.py` regenerates the inventory using exclusive creation; move the existing owned output aside before any intentional rerun.

## Scalar-head certification

`inventory/b8_other_names_accepted.json` narrows the review set to 26 heads, totaling 41 bytes. The screening additionally requires the legacy DAT inventory to agree on the exact scalar type, checks natural alignment and one data-v3 chunk, rejects interior unit names or data labels and intersecting saved fields or relocation cells, and excludes recorded Batch 7, data-v1, produced and rewritten-directory ranges. Three previously proposed `char` heads are deferred because the legacy inventory lacks a corroborating type. These checks use the recorded inputs and cannot establish that the rewritten-directory list is exhaustive.

An isolated temporary object compiled the 26 exact scalar initializers with VC2008 SP1 and distinct `M3_` names. The actual `splice.slice_entry` reader confirmed reference bytes, expected section, external storage, and zero relocations for every head; the object had no dynamic initializer. The temporary object was discarded. The existing scalar generator assigns C++ `DAT_` names, which do not represent the captured `extern "C"` source linkage. Integration must use the tested distinct names or correctly implement the source linkage, and must still perform the full image build, controls, and gameplay checks. No interval tails are selected.

The certification and isolated object check are reproduced by `b8_other_names_certify.py` and `b8_other_names_compilecheck.py`; the former creates its output exclusively and will not overwrite the saved inventory.
