# Five-header adoption impact

The bounded source/include census is `out/phase2-bgsbodypart-adoption-census/census.json`, generated by `bgsbodypart_adoption_census.py` against its recorded unit-recipe hash. It scans only `fnv_decomp/src` and `fnv_decomp/include`. It treats every existing quoted-include search candidate as reachable, without preprocessing; comments, macros, namespaces and inactive branches can produce false positives. Counts identify review candidates, not proven global ODR conflicts or required source edits.

The lexical census finds 171 recipe units with at least one definition of one of the five names in their possible include closure: 151 include a TESModel definition, 72 a FileEntryList definition, 18 a BGSBodyPart definition, two a BodyPartInfo definition, and one a TESModelPSA definition. Categories overlap. The unit and source-file inventory, line numbers, and source hashes are in the JSON. Six units have multiple same-name definition hits within the conservative closure, but those hits need namespace and preprocessor resolution before they can be called same-scope redefinitions.

Unit 620's direct closure defines BodyPartInfo, TESModel and BGSBodyPart in `src/fallout_shared/bgsbodypart_decls.h`. Unit 621's direct closure defines all five names in `src/fallout_shared/bgsbodypartdata.h`. The proposed five headers can be introduced in those two units only after their local class definitions and transitive includes are reconciled; the private split fixture tests this two-unit hypothesis but does not establish compatibility with the other units.

Other BGSBodyPart-definition leads are units 55–57, 253, 336, 370, 423–425, 576, 933–936, 1082 and 2638. They include header wrappers, optimized variants and local source views; exact namespaces, whether they instantiate or access fields, and method declarations must be checked individually before adopting a canonical header. Unit 253 and unit 1082 each contain two lexical BGSBodyPart hits. Unit 1037 has three FileEntryList hits, and unit 1132 has two TESModel hits; these may be separate namespaces or adapters. Their mere presence does not authorize wholesale replacement.

The breadth of the TESModel and FileEntryList closures is the main adoption risk. For an actual payload, trace compiler-resolved include paths and preprocessor state for each affected unit; distinguish native global classes from namespaced layout views; compare data layout, base and virtual signatures, special members, use sites and all mapped functions before replacement. Keep synthetic adapters separately named where source evidence only supports a view. Preserve the full 620/621 15+10, EH, RTTI and vtable gates, then expand verification to every unit whose source or resolved include changes. Do not infer full native-class recovery from the two-unit split fixture.
